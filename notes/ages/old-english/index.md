Old English writing grew in the kingdoms formed after Germanic settlement in Britain. Its language is very different from present-day English, and much of what survives was copied in Christian monasteries. The period closes with the Norman Conquest of 1066, a political turning point rather than a sudden end to every Old English tradition.

## Poetry and manuscripts

*Beowulf* is the best-known heroic poem. Its battles with Grendel, Grendel's mother and a dragon sit beside questions about loyalty, fame and death. Other surviving poems include the elegies *The Wanderer* and *The Seafarer*, and the religious poem *The Dream of the Rood*. Alliterative verse links stressed words by sound rather than by end rhyme. The main poetic witnesses are the Beowulf manuscript, the Exeter Book, the Vercelli Book and the Junius manuscript.

## Writers and learning

Caedmon is associated with an early religious hymn; Cynewulf is one of the few Old English poets whose name survives in the poems themselves. Alfred the Great supported translation and learning, while later prose writers such as Aelfric and Wulfstan used English for teaching and preaching. For exams, distinguish the date of a surviving manuscript from the date a work was first composed.
