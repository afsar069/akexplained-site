The period after 1945 includes postmodern experiments, new voices from formerly colonised societies and many kinds of writing that do not fit a single movement. "Postmodern" is therefore a useful approach to some works, but it should not be treated as the name of every book published since the Second World War.

## Different directions

Samuel Beckett's plays, including *Waiting for Godot*, challenge expectations about plot, speech and theatrical action. Philip Larkin's poems often use a plain surface to examine disappointment and everyday life; they are not all examples of postmodern technique. Salman Rushdie's *Midnight's Children* joins history, storytelling and multiple cultural traditions in a way that invites questions about who tells a nation's story.

## Reading with care

Ask whether a work questions stable narrative authority, mixes forms or revisits earlier stories; then explain how it does so. Avoid attaching the label automatically to a writer.
