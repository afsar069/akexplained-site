The Augustan label points to writers' interest in classical literary models and to their own comparisons with the Rome of Augustus. In early eighteenth-century Britain, a growing print market brought essays, satire and poetry to readers in coffeehouses and homes.

## Satire in verse and prose

Alexander Pope used the heroic couplet with great precision in *The Rape of the Lock* and *The Dunciad*. Jonathan Swift's *Gulliver's Travels* uses imaginary voyages to expose human pride and political folly. Joseph Addison and Richard Steele helped shape the periodical essay through *The Spectator*. These works differ in form, but all address a public world of argument and judgement.

## Reading the age

Do not treat "Augustan" as a synonym for all eighteenth-century literature. Later writers placed more emphasis on feeling, sympathy and the individual response. Keep the title and publication period of a work in view when making comparisons. The [full Augustan guide](/notes/augustan-age/) covers the age's society, genres and main writers.
