The two world wars and rapid social change form the background to this timeline's Modern period. Writers experimented with form because older, continuous ways of telling experience often seemed inadequate. The 1914–1945 dates are a teaching frame; Modernism began earlier and its effects lasted later.

## New forms

T. S. Eliot's *The Waste Land* moves among voices, quotations and fragments. Virginia Woolf's *Mrs Dalloway* follows thought and memory through a single day. James Joyce's *Ulysses* reworks an ancient epic in a modern city. These works do not share one technique, but they ask readers to pay close attention to point of view, time and the act of reading itself.

## A useful comparison

Set a Modernist text beside an Edwardian realist work or a Georgian lyric to see that several styles existed at once. Do not date every twentieth-century writer by the period's two wars alone. The [full Modern Age guide](/notes/modern-age/) covers writing from 1901 to 1945 in more detail.
