The English Renaissance is a broad cultural period, not one royal reign. Classical learning, humanist education, religious change and a growing print trade shaped writing from the early Tudors through the first half of the seventeenth century. The timeline's 1500–1660 span includes several narrower ages with different political settings.

## A changing literary culture

Thomas More's *Utopia* belongs to the humanist world of the early Tudors. Wyatt and Surrey adapted continental lyric forms into English. Under Elizabeth I, Sidney, Spenser, Marlowe and Shakespeare helped expand lyric and drama. After 1603, Donne, Jonson and Webster developed different poetic and theatrical styles. Milton's early work and the writing of the Civil War years show how far the period extends beyond Elizabeth's death.

## How to study the umbrella period

Use "Renaissance" for the broad movement, and use Early Tudor, Elizabethan, Jacobean, Caroline or Commonwealth when a question asks about a narrower moment. The [Renaissance guide](/notes/renaissance/) concentrates on 1500–1603; the [Jacobean and Caroline guide](/notes/jacobean-and-caroline-age/) continues the story into the seventeenth century.
