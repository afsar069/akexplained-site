Romantic writing placed new emphasis on imagination, nature and the individual's experience. The dates 1785–1832 are one teaching convention; the 1798 publication of *Lyrical Ballads* is another common starting point. Literary movements do not change overnight at either boundary.

## Poetry and imagination

William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge's *Lyrical Ballads* challenged expectations about poetic subjects and language. Wordsworth often explored memory and encounters with nature; Coleridge used supernatural settings and psychological uncertainty in poems such as *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner*. John Keats's odes joined intense sensory detail with questions about beauty and time. Shelley and Byron offered other, often more openly political, Romantic voices.

## A wider field

Romantic writing includes prose and fiction as well as poetry. Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* connects scientific ambition with responsibility and isolation. When comparing texts, name the writer and work rather than treating "Romantic" as one fixed style. The [full Romantic guide](/notes/romantic-age/) gives the wider period and its sources.
