Privacy Policy

An account is optional. You can read every page and take the quizzes without one. If you make an account, this page says exactly what is kept.

The short version

You do not need an account to use this site. Every article, the daily quiz and the MCQs are open to everyone, and you can read all of them without telling me anything about yourself. If you choose to make a free account, I keep your name, your email address, the path you choose, and a record of the quiz questions you answer while you are logged in, so that the site can remember your work. There is no newsletter and no advertising, and visitors who do not make an account are not tracked.

What is collected

  • Nothing, when you are simply reading. No cookies set by me, no advertising network, no behavioural profile. Your quiz progress is saved in your own browser (its local storage) so that you can carry on later. It stays on your device and is not sent to me.
  • Standard server records. The hosting provider, Cloudflare, keeps ordinary technical logs — the pages requested, approximate region, browser type. This is how every website on the internet works and I use it only to see which pages are read.
  • What you type into the contact form. Your name, email address and message are sent to my inbox so that I can reply. That is the only reason they are collected.
  • If you make an account. Your name and email address. A password, if you sign up with email; it is kept only in a scrambled form that cannot be turned back into your password. The path or paths you choose, such as an exam or a degree. Any “notify me” request you make. And, only while you are logged in, a record of each quiz question you answer: which question it was, whether you were right, which quiz it came from, and when. Answers given when you are not logged in are never sent anywhere.
  • A login token on your device. While you are logged in, your browser keeps a small token in its local storage so that you stay logged in. Logging out removes it.

Signing in with Google

You can sign in with Google instead of a password. This is optional. If you do, Google tells this site your name, your email address and the link to your Google profile picture. I use the name and the email address; I do not show or use the picture. I never see your Google password. What Google does with its own data is covered by Google’s privacy policy.

If you already have an account with the same email address, signing in with Google opens that same account.

The list of top students

At the end of an MCQ test, a logged-in student sees a short list of the top ten students. It shows each of them by the name on their account and how accurate their answers have been, and nothing else. Your own place on that list is marked for you.

This means that if you reach the top ten, the name on your account and your accuracy are visible to other logged-in students. Nothing else about you is shown: not your email address, not how many questions you answered, not which questions you answered, not when you were here. A student outside the top ten is never named to anyone.

You join the list only after answering 100 different questions. If you would rather not appear on it, you can change the name on your account on My Account, or write to me and I will remove your account.

What is not done

  • Your email address is never added to a mailing list.
  • Nothing is sold, shared or passed to any third party for marketing. This includes everything held in an account.
  • There is no advertising on this site and no advertiser has any relationship with it.
  • You will never receive a message from this site that you did not ask for.

Third-party services

Five outside services are involved, and it is only fair that you know:

  • Cloudflare hosts the site and serves the pages.
  • Supabase holds accounts, chosen paths and quiz records, for people who make an account.
  • Google supplies the two typefaces used here (Google Fonts), and provides the optional Google sign-in.
  • Resend is our email provider. It sends the emails an account needs, the sign-up confirmation and the password reset, to people who make an account.
  • Web3Forms passes contact-form messages to my email.

Links to outside websites — Project Gutenberg, the Poetry Foundation, YouTube and others — take you to services with their own policies, which I do not control.

Removing your information

If you have written to me and want your message and email address deleted from my inbox, ask and it will be done. The same goes for an account: ask, and your account and everything saved with it — your name, email address, paths and quiz record — will be deleted. No explanation required, no form to fill.
