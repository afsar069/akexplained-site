# Key Terms & Concepts: what is in the series, and what each chapter rests on

Written 2026-11-25 for the owner and for any later session. The series is `_data/keyTerms.js`
(8 parts, 40 chapters, 271 terms, every chapter Coming soon). Rule 2 applies: nothing here is invented, and what
could not be checked is said plainly.

## What the NET syllabus says (read 2026-11-25)

The official UGC NET Bureau syllabus for **English, code 30** lists only unit titles. **It has no list of
literary terms.** There is no official "key terms" breakdown for NET, so the owner's request to build this
section "in the same format" is met from the owner's own taxonomy, not from an official text.

One weaker NET link exists, from one coaching site (limitlessliterature.com; its pages are study-guide
material, not NTA's): its page of past questions on language, linguistics and prosody counts how often a
device came up. It was used only to see that terms of prosody and figures of speech really are asked
(for example metre, foot and scansion; the five feet; rhyme and stanza forms; sonnet, ode and elegy;
metaphor, synecdoche and metonymy; chiasmus, zeugma, oxymoron). The counts were **not** used to pick or
drop any term. Soliloquy and aside had no hits on that page, and they stay, because the taxonomy holds them.

## What the structure rests on instead

**The owner's master taxonomy**, `supabase/data/taxonomy-master-reference.csv` (rule 11: "Invent no topics, the
owner supplies them"):

- the area **Literary Terms and Devices**: 8 group nodes and **198 terms** under them;
- **76 `concept` nodes** in the area Literary Criticism and Theory (mimesis, catharsis, rasa, dhvani, defamiliarization,
  the signifier, differance, hegemony, the implied reader, the subaltern, hyperreality and so on).

That is 274 nodes. The series holds **271 terms**, because **three ideas are in both lists**: *Decorum* and *The Sublime*
(each is a term under "rhetoric and criticism terms" in the devices area and also a concept under Horace and
Longinus in the criticism area) and *Archetype* (*Archetype (literary)* in the devices area, *Archetype* in the
criticism area). Each is listed once: Decorum and The Sublime in Part 7, Classical Criticism, and Archetype in
Part 8, Psychoanalysis and Myth. A script (2026-11-25) matched every one of the 271 terms to a taxonomy name and
found **no term missing from the series, none added from outside, and none twice**. Three names are shown without the
bracket the taxonomy adds (it is only a gloss): *Alankara (Figuration)* shows as Alankara, *Aucitya (Propriety)* as
Aucitya, *Dramatic Monologue (form)* as Dramatic Monologue.

## What is the owner's and what is Claude's

- **Terms:** all from the taxonomy. None added.
- **Grouping into parts and chapters, and every title and summary:** an **editorial grouping made by Claude** to make
  198 + 76 terms readable (the taxonomy's own 8 groups were regrouped into figures of speech, sound and metre,
  forms, genres, drama and narrative, and rhetoric). The owner may rename, merge or move any of it, as with the other
  series. It follows no official syllabus and the page does not claim one.
- **Thinkers lines (`people`):** only on the chapters of Parts 7 and 8, and only names that the taxonomy gives as the
  **parent of one of that chapter's terms** (checked by script). A chapter of Parts 1 to 6 has no thinkers line,
  because the taxonomy names no person for those terms (one exception, *Comedy of Humours*, whose parent is Ben
  Jonson, was left off the line on purpose, to keep the card short; add it if the owner wants).
- **Terms already home elsewhere:** *Decorum*, *The Sublime* and *Archetype* are placed once, in the criticism and
  theory parts (Parts 7 and 8). The Indian poetics terms (Part 7, chapter 2) are a short list of the nodes the taxonomy holds; the
  full treatment of each school is in the Indian Poetics series, and the articles should link to it.

## Term buttons and how a term opens (owner, 2026-11-25)

Every term in a chapter card is a **full-width strip with its name in the middle**; clicking it opens that term's own article.
The owner's words: "ye jo list hai, unke buton bana do, ek patti jo puri idhar se udhar, uske beech naam, click karne se seedha
article khulega." This replaced the bullet list the other series still use.

- **One article per term**, at `/notes/term-<name>/`. The address is made from the term's name by `termSlug()` in
  `_data/keyTerms.js`: lower case, plain letters, hyphens, brackets kept as words. Examples: Simile -> `term-simile`;
  The Nine Rasas (Navarasa) -> `term-the-nine-rasas-navarasa`; Ethos, Pathos and Logos -> `term-ethos-pathos-and-logos`.
  The build stops if two terms make the same address (a check shows 271 different addresses today). In Pages CMS the
  owner makes the article with the folder name `term-<name>`.
- **No article yet = a plain strip, no link**, so there is no 404. All 271 are like this today. A screen reader hears
  "(article coming soon)" after the name.
- **To open a term:** put its exact name in the `liveTerms` list at the top of the code in `_data/keyTerms.js`. The build
  then checks that the name is a real term and that `notes/term-<name>/index.md` exists, and says so in plain words if not.
  The strip turns teal and links to the article, and the chapter card's foot reads "N of M articles ready".
  If an article must live at another address, add it to `termSlugOverrides` in the same file.
- **Colours (owner, 2026-11-25: "button ka thoda colour change karo, jab cursor jaae to aur change ho"):** a strip without an article is soft teal (`#DCEEE9`, dark teal name) and turns a deeper teal tint (`#B7DCD3`) when the cursor is over it, with a normal cursor; a strip with an article is solid teal (`--teal`, white name) and turns darker (`--teal-dark`) with a small lift and a pointer. So more colour means the term opens. Name contrast is 5.5 or higher in all four states. Rules `.ltx-term*` in `assets/site.css`.
- **Why the chapter card does not link:** the terms are the articles, and a link inside a link is invalid, so the card
  around the strips is only a box. A chapter's own `slug` and `live` are not used for Key Terms.
- Checked 2026-11-25 with a temporary "Simile" article (removed again): the strip links to it, the build refuses a term
  that has no article, 271 strips at 1440, 800 and 375 px, none cut off, none narrower than the card, no nested links.
- Only Key Terms has strips. Indian Poetics, Cultural Studies and Literary Theory keep their plain lists, because their
  subchapters are sections of a chapter article, not articles of their own. Ask the owner before changing that.

## Cautions for the articles

- Several terms are used differently by different schools (irony, the Other, ideology, the sublime, genre
  names such as romance and satire). Each article should say whose meaning it uses and cite its source.
- Terms with a **writer's own source** (Aristotle's *Poetics*, Horace's *Ars Poetica*, Longinus's *On the Sublime*,
  Saussure's *Course in General Linguistics*, Derrida's essays, Lacan's *Écrits*) should be quoted from a reliable
  edition, not from a study guide.
- *Aucitya*, *Sphota*, *Riti* and the other Sanskrit terms: transliterations differ between sources; the spelling here
  is the taxonomy's. Check the Indian Poetics notes before writing.
- Nothing here is an official NET list. Do not describe any chapter as "NET syllabus" on the site.
