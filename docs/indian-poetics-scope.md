# Indian Poetics: what is in the series, and what each chapter rests on

Written 2026-11-25 for the owner and for any later session. The series is `_data/indianPoetics.js`
(7 parts, 35 chapters, 158 subchapters, every chapter Coming soon). Rule 2 applies: nothing here is invented,
and what could not be checked is said plainly.

## What the NET syllabus says (read 2026-11-25)

The official UGC NET Bureau syllabus for **English, code 30** lists only unit titles. **Indian poetics is not
named anywhere in it.** Unit VIII is just "Literary Criticism". So there is no official breakdown of Indian
poetics for NET, and the owner's request "according to the NET syllabus" cannot be met from an official text.
Our own `/exams/net/syllabus/` page does not mention Indian aesthetics under Unit VIII either (its text is
labelled "study-guide notes").

Two weaker NET links exist, both from one coaching site, limitlessliterature.com:

- Its own Unit VIII line (its list of what has returned in past papers, not NTA's): *"Indian aesthetics: rasa,
  dhvani, alankara, vakrokti, auchitya and their theorists."*
- Its page of **10** past "Indian Aesthetics" questions. They are mostly about Sanskrit drama and dramatists (Bhasa,
  Kalidasa, Bhavabhuti's *Uttararamacharita*), the number of forms of drama in Bharata's manual, Bhartrhari's
  *sphota*, and the order of texts. Rasa, dhvani, alankara, riti, vakrokti and aucitya come up only once or twice in
  the whole page. So the evidence that NET asks about poetics proper is thin.

## What the structure rests on instead

**UGC ePG Pathshala** (INFLIBNET), the UGC's own open course for an English paper, read in full on 2026-11-25:

- *Indian Literary Criticism and Theory* (`ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/engp11/`), 34 modules. Chapters and subchapters
  here follow its modules and the section headings inside them.
- *Indian aesthetics and fine arts* (`ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/icp04/`), used for Mammata and Vishwanatha
  ("The place of Kavyaprakasa and Sahityadarpana in Poetics"), "Interpretations of Rasasutra", "Ten types of Sanskrit
  Drama" and "Schools of Poetry".

The four parts also match how one university syllabus splits the subject (C.U. Shah University, MA English
Sem II, "Indian Poetics", found through search and not opened as a page): Rasa, Alamkara and Riti in one unit;
Dhvani, Vakrokti and Auchitya in the next. Our hub's wording and the master taxonomy
(`supabase/data/taxonomy-master-reference.csv`, node `indian-poetics`) already named the same writers and concepts.

## Cautions for the articles

- Parts 5 to 7 (ePG modules on medieval, modern and contemporary thought) have no NET evidence behind them; they follow the UGC course. Several modules are as much about literary history or a writer's life as about poetics, and some carry typos in names; the series corrects the spellings, and each article must check its facts.

- The ePG modules are course texts, not primary sources, and a few have damaged diacritics or typos. Check each fact
  against a standard history (P. V. Kane, S. K. De) or a translation before it goes into an article. No dates are
  stated on the site pages for this reason.
- Sources disagree on some points: six schools or eight; whether *auchitya* is the "soul" or the "life" of poetry;
  who first said riti is the soul of poetry (Dandin's framework, made explicit by Vamana). Say so in the article.
- Chapter 11's part summary says Abhinavagupta joined rasa and dhvani; that comes from a search summary and the
  Locana link, not from a module that says it in those words.
